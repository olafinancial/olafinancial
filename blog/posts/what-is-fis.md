# What is the Financial Independence Score (FIS)?

**FIS** is Pul Planning’s **primary KPI** — the number we want you to understand before anything else.

## The idea in one line

> **FIS = how much of your monthly outflows are covered by passive income.**

When FIS hits **100%**, passive income (dividends, rent, pensions, and similar) can cover the lifestyle costs you’ve logged — the classic meaning of *financial independence* for everyday planning.

## Simple formula

```
FIS (%) = (Monthly passive income ÷ Monthly outflows) × 100
```

| Range | Meaning (Pul labels) |
|-------|----------------------|
| **Under 50%** | Building — passive income still covers a small share of outflows |
| **50–99%** | Halfway there — keep growing passive income and controlling costs |
| **100%+** | Financially independent on the numbers you’ve entered |

## What counts as “passive”?

In Pul, passive-style income typically includes things like:

- Dividends and interest  
- Rental income  
- Pension / RSA-style draws you treat as passive  
- Other streams you categorise as passive (not your main salary)

Your **active salary** still matters for cash flow and tax — but FIS deliberately asks: *“If I only had non-active income, could I fund my life?”*

## How to improve FIS

1. **Log outflows honestly** — FIS is only as good as your expense data  
2. **Grow passive streams** carefully (investments, rentals, etc.)  
3. **Cut or restructure fixed costs** so the same passive income covers more  
4. Use the in-app **FI Calculator** for 25×-style projections and years-to-FI scenarios
